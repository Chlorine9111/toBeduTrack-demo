1. The Foundations of Liberty

Rome was not built in a day, nor was its Republic. Following the expulsion of the last Etruscan king, Tarquinius Superbus, in 509 BCE, the Roman people established a system designed to prevent the concentration of absolute power in any single individual.

Central to this system were the two consuls, elected annually, who shared executive power and could veto one another's decisions. This "checks and balances" approach would later influence modern democratic structures across the globe.
