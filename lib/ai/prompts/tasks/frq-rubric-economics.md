### Economics FRQ Scoring Notes
Use AP-style scoring that rewards correct model choice, accurate graph or quantitative setup, and a complete causal chain in economic language.

#### Point design
- Separate points should reflect distinct acts: identifying the correct market or model, setting up the graph or calculation, tracing the causal chain, and stating the final economic outcome.
- Award graph points only when axes, curves, initial equilibrium, and direction of change are labeled clearly enough to support the conclusion.
- Award verbal reasoning points only when the student explains why the change occurs, not just what changes.
- If the question is computational, correct setup and correct arithmetic should be scored separately when possible.

#### Common scoreable acts
| Skill | What earns the point |
| --- | --- |
| Model selection | Chooses the correct framework such as supply-demand, market structure, money market, loanable funds, or AD-AS |
| Graph or setup accuracy | Labels axes and curves correctly, identifies equilibrium, and shows the correct shift or formula setup |
| Causal transmission | Explains the mechanism linking the initial change to price, quantity, output, unemployment, inflation, or welfare |
| Correct conclusion | States the final economic effect using the correct direction and variable |
| Contextual interpretation | Connects the result back to consumers, firms, government, or macro indicators when the prompt requires it |

#### Partial-credit guidance
- Give setup credit when the student identifies the right graph or equation even if a later sign or arithmetic error occurs.
- Allow follow-through credit when later reasoning is internally consistent with the student's earlier mistake, except where the mistaken setup destroys the target concept.
- Separate graph-description credit from policy-evaluation credit; a student may earn one without the other.
- Require explicit economic vocabulary such as shortage, equilibrium, real GDP, inflationary pressure, marginal benefit, or deadweight loss when those ideas are central.

#### No-credit patterns
- No credit for a graph with unlabeled or mislabeled axes when the graph itself is the scored evidence.
- No credit for a conclusion stated without the mechanism that produces it when the prompt asks for explanation.
- No credit for mixing short-run and long-run logic without signaling the transition.
- No credit for confusing nominal and real measures when that distinction is central to the task.

Before finalizing:
- Check that each point corresponds to a distinct economic act rather than a general impression of "good understanding."
- Check that the rubric does not reward diagram drawing alone without correct economic reasoning.
