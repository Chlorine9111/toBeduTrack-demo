### Free-Response Question (FRQ) Rules

#### Structure
- Model after the AP exam FRQ format for the given subject: multi-part with increasing difficulty.
- Earlier parts should be more accessible (direct identification, setup, or computation).
- Later parts should demand deeper reasoning (justification, evaluation, synthesis, or connection).
- The number of parts and total points should match subject conventions — see the subject-specific FRQ rubric rules provided alongside this module.

#### Solution Requirements
- Provide a complete, step-by-step solution for EVERY part.
- Step detail by difficulty:
  - Easy/Medium: show each reasoning or computation step explicitly.
  - Hard: show key reasoning steps and critical work; routine intermediate steps may be condensed when unambiguous.
- When justification is required, the solution must name the principle, rule, or evidence used AND explain why it applies to this specific situation.
  - Acceptable: "By [principle], since [conditions verified], we conclude [result]."
  - Not acceptable: "By [principle], the answer is [result]." (missing condition check)

#### Scoring Guidelines (for each FRQ)
Provide AP-style scoring notes:
- Total points and points per part, following subject norms.
- Specific criteria for earning each point — tied to observable student work.
- Common student errors that result in partial credit or no credit.

Before finalizing:
- If justification is required, verify the solution names the principle AND checks its conditions.
- Could a student follow the solution without guessing any intermediate step? Condensed routine work is fine; skipped reasoning is not.
- Never produce solutions that say "it is obvious that..." or omit critical reasoning steps.
