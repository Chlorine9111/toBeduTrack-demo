### English FRQ Scoring Notes
Use AP-style scoring that rewards a defensible line of interpretation, specific textual evidence, and commentary that explains how language choices create meaning.

#### Point design
- Separate points should distinguish thesis, evidence, commentary, and higher-order sophistication when the task warrants it.
- Award evidence points only when the student uses specific quoted or paraphrased details from the passage.
- Award commentary points only when the student explains how the cited language functions in relation to the claim.
- Treat device spotting and analysis as different acts; naming a rhetorical or literary device without explaining effect is not enough.

#### Common scoreable acts
| Skill | What earns the point |
| --- | --- |
| Defensible thesis | Presents a clear interpretive or argumentative claim that answers the prompt directly |
| Specific evidence | Uses accurate textual references, quotations, or paraphrases tied to the relevant lines or moments |
| Commentary | Explains how diction, syntax, imagery, structure, tone, or reasoning supports the thesis |
| Line of reasoning | Organizes claims so that paragraphs build an argument rather than a list of observations |
| Sophistication when warranted | Sustains complexity, nuance, tension, qualification, or broader significance without drifting off prompt |

#### Partial-credit guidance
- Give evidence credit only when the textual detail is relevant to the claim being made.
- Give commentary credit only when the student explains effect, purpose, or meaning; summary alone does not count.
- Allow a student to earn strong evidence/commentary credit even if the prose style is not polished, as long as the analysis is clear and accurate.
- Reserve sophistication credit for genuinely complex thinking sustained across the response, not for elevated vocabulary or a single abstract sentence.

#### No-credit patterns
- No credit for plot summary or passage paraphrase with no interpretive claim.
- No credit for device identification without explanation of function.
- No credit for outside biographical or historical information unless the prompt explicitly invites it.
- No credit for contradictory claims about tone, purpose, or theme that the essay never reconciles.

Before finalizing:
- Make sure each rubric point can be awarded from observable features in the essay.
- Make sure the rubric rewards analysis of the provided text, not generic writing quality alone.
