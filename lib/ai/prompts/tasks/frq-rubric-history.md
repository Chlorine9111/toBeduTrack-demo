### History FRQ Scoring Notes
Use AP-style scoring that rewards defensible historical argumentation, specific evidence use, and disciplined reasoning with time period and context.

#### Point design
- Separate points should map to distinct historical acts such as thesis/claim, contextualization, use of evidence, sourcing, comparison, causation, or continuity/change reasoning.
- Award evidence points only when the student uses a specific historical fact, document detail, or example to support a claim.
- Award sourcing or contextualization points only when the student explains why the source situation, audience, purpose, or broader context matters to the argument.
- Treat document summary and argument as different acts; summary alone is not analysis.

#### Common scoreable acts
| Skill | What earns the point |
| --- | --- |
| Defensible claim | States a historically supportable thesis that answers the prompt directly |
| Contextualization | Situates the argument within broader historical developments in the relevant period |
| Evidence use | Names and deploys specific events, policies, groups, or document details in service of the argument |
| Sourcing or perspective analysis | Explains how author purpose, audience, point of view, or situation shapes meaning |
| Historical reasoning | Uses causation, comparison, or continuity/change logic explicitly rather than listing facts |

#### Partial-credit guidance
- Give credit for evidence only when it is tied to the student's line of reasoning, not just dropped into the paragraph.
- Allow partial credit when the student identifies the right historical development but explains the relationship incompletely.
- Do not award the same fact twice under two categories unless it performs two clearly different argumentative functions.
- If the prompt is document-based, distinguish between citing the document and analyzing the document.

#### No-credit patterns
- No credit for vague theses such as "many factors contributed" without a specific claim.
- No credit for evidence that falls outside the relevant timeframe unless the task explicitly asks for broader comparison.
- No credit for document paraphrase that never explains significance.
- No credit for unsupported moral judgment or present-day opinion posed as historical analysis.

Before finalizing:
- Make sure every point can be justified by a specific sentence or analytical move in student writing.
- Make sure the rubric rewards argument quality and evidence use, not mere length or number of facts.
