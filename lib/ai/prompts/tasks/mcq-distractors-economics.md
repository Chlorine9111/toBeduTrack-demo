### Economics MCQ Distractor Rules
Each distractor MUST come from a specific economic misconception or broken reasoning chain.
Use at least 2 distinct categories per question from:

| Category | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Curve direction error | Shifting demand left when the scenario changes supply |
| Broken transmission chain | Jumping from interest rates directly to GDP without the investment step |
| Short-run or long-run confusion | Applying short-run Phillips Curve logic to a long-run outcome |
| Nominal versus real confusion | Treating a nominal increase as proof of a real increase |
| Micro versus macro confusion | Using an individual market rule to explain an aggregate outcome |
| Policy sign reversal | Describing contractionary fiscal policy as expansionary |

#### Subject-specific requirements
- Keep every option within the same model family as the stem: supply-demand, market structure, AD-AS, money market, Phillips Curve, etc.
- Distractors should usually preserve the same variables or graph axes but move the wrong curve, predict the wrong equilibrium change, or break the causal chain.
- If the item is quantitative, wrong answers should follow from realistic setup mistakes, not arbitrary numbers.
- If the item is qualitative, each option should name a concrete economic effect on price, quantity, output, unemployment, inflation, surplus, or welfare.

#### Anti-patterns
- Do not use policy statements so vague that more than one answer could be defended.
- Do not mix micro and macro vocabulary carelessly just to create variation.
- Do not write distractors that ignore the stated ceteris paribus condition, timeframe, or market structure.

Before finalizing:
- Trace the correct answer step by step through the model and verify that each distractor breaks at a different step.
- Confirm that the graph direction, economic sign, and final verbal conclusion are all consistent with one another.
