### English MCQ Distractor Rules
Each distractor MUST correspond to a specific reading or interpretation error that students plausibly make.
Use at least 2 distinct categories per question from:

| Category | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Device misidentification | Calling a metaphor a simile when no comparative marker appears |
| Overreading author intent | Treating descriptive language as proof of endorsement or condemnation |
| Argument structure error | Confusing concession, counterargument, and refutation |
| Evidence scope error | Using background knowledge instead of details from the passage |
| Tone misread | Reading irony or restraint as sincere praise |
| Sequence-for-causation error | Treating adjacent events or ideas as a causal chain without textual support |

#### Subject-specific requirements
- Every option must remain anchored to the provided passage. Students should need to read the language closely, not rely on outside facts.
- Distractors should usually reuse a real textual feature but interpret its function, tone, or significance incorrectly.
- Wrong answers may sound sophisticated, but they must fail because they misread wording, structure, evidence, or rhetorical purpose.
- When the question asks about a line or paragraph, distractors should stay local to that textual range unless the skill being tested explicitly requires a broader structural view.

#### Anti-patterns
- Do not create a distractor that could be defended by quoting a different part of the text.
- Do not make the correct answer the only option that actually references the passage.
- Do not reward test-taking tricks such as picking the most abstract or the most cautious wording.

Before finalizing:
- Match each wrong option to a concrete misreading of diction, syntax, imagery, structure, or evidence use.
- Re-read the passage and ask whether each choice can be supported from the assigned lines. If more than one can, revise.
